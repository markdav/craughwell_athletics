Jamie Fallon had an outstanding run at the National Marathon Championships in Dublin on Sunday.  The popular Craughwell man has been an ever-present on the roads around east Galway snce a young age and he finally made his first venture over the 26.22 mile distance.  In wet and breezy conditions, Jamie ran the entire way at a superb 3:19 per km, to complete the distance in 2:19.37, for a massive new Club Record.  Jamie was 7th Irishman and 14th person overall home.  

Well done also to all the Craughwell AC finishers, including previous club record holder Kevin Mooney 2:43, Mary Woulfe (3:44), Sharon Gilligan (3:51), Maeve Hynes, Maura Walsh, Laura Porter, Fiona Keane, Jamie's Mum Irene Fallon and Reena Deely among others!  

Back home, the Connacht Even Ages Cross Country took place in wet and windy Enniscrone on a beautiful and tough course that had everything.  The day started with  the U10 girls where Katie Considine once again was in fine form, taking a clear win. She was followed by Lucy McGovern (14), Lara McGovern (15), Aoibhin Grace Gallagher McKeon (32), Kate Frazer and Caoimhe Hall to seal a fine Bronze for the village girls.  Billy O'Brien (13th), Gabriel Killoran (27) and Alex McGovern (50) all ran strongly in the u10 boys race.

It was another brilliant individual Gold for the U12s as Ciara Ward (1), Leah O'Toole (5), Olivia Rooney (53) and Freya Wholihan (54) ran exceptionally well.  

Muireann Kelly (13), Emma Mc Govern (14) and Laoise Corban (23) impressed but were just one girl shy of a team in the U14 girls.  

David Frazer broke clear with the talented Swinford Athlete Rory Gruddy in the U16 Boys race and the pair had a fascinating over and back battle over the 4km distance with David just missing out on Gold by a few paces.  David led the Galwaymen home to Silver.

Cathal Gallagher impressed in the U18 boys race which was mixed with the Junior men.  Cathal finished 4th in a very stacked field where Galway were dominant winners.    Mathys Bocquet almost missed his race as they unexpectedly combined two races, and he slipped and fell at the start.  He ran strongly to come home 2nd in the Junior men, but didn't really feel like he got into the race.  In the Senior men, the ever improving Fergus Kelly ran outstandingly to come 10th among the 44 hardy competitors.
