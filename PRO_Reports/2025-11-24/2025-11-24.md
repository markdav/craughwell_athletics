A fantastic David Frazer Silver was the highlight of another huge day for Craughwell AC, full of ups and downs at the National Even-ages Cross Country in Derry.

The course consisted of laps of pitches and an adjoining field at the Templemore Sports Complex on the outskirts of Derry.  We knew it would be flat, but the weather of the day and the weeks prior made it very wet and mucky all throughout the course.  When you add to that the biting cold wind and showers of sleet and rain, it's safe to say that hot showers and comfort food were in high demand after.

In the under-16 Boys race, teak-tough David Frazer was Galway's big hope.  David has had a great year, coming 3rd in the Schools All-Ireland but just missed out on international selection by a few days due to his birthdate.  An added incentive for this race was that the top-4 in the U16s race would qualify for the Celtic Cross Country event in Feb 2026 at the famous Parliament Hill, near London.  David started the race hard, as was necessary with the long starting straight, finding himself in about 8th place after the first shorter 1km lap.  The eventual race winner Darragh Whelan is a potentially generational talent and he stormed off at this point, leaving the rest to race for two medals and 3 Irish cap positions over the final two 1.5km longer and muddier laps.   In the second lap, the field had stretched further with David hanging in 7th and a couple of more break away athletes attempting to close the gap.  With 1500m to go David's fitness started to show as he moved up, catching one after another until he found himself in 4th with 150m to run where he put on an almighty finishing kick to reel in those ahead of him and win a superb individual Silver and an International cap.  

Last year was a special day for the Craughwell Senior team as we finished 5th and William won an historic National Senior Cap.  Unfortunately this year was not quite as successful, but it was still a fine performance overall to finish in 9th place and once again Connacht's top club.  The standard at the front is always sky-high but was possibly the strongest field in recent memory.

This time, Oisín Davis was first Galway man home in 9th U23, 25th overall.  Oisín is busy with studies and wasn't feeling great, but still ran a strong race to back up his performance of last year with the same finishing position in a stronger field.  William Fitzgerald was next Craughwell man home in 37th.  After a rapid start, William found himself leading the chase pack and made an almighty effort to bridge the gap to the leaders. This effort ultimately cost him badly later in the race.  Jack Miskella ran really well to finish in 88th place - Jack got his pacing slightly wrong and lost a lot of places on the last lap.  Jordan Walsh was 129th and Anthony Boyle back from illness next in 143rd place.

Craughwell had a strong team in the U20 category where the U18s and the U20s combined over 4500m.  Mathys Bocquet led the team home in 23rd place overall, gaining position quickly in the last lap but running out of ground.  Cathal Gallagher was next home in 50th, (20th over 18), closely followed by the fast-finishing Declan O'Connell in 51st.  Luke O'Sullivan was quite ill bravely finished the race out for the sake of the team.  Cathal and Declan scored on the Galway U18 team won Silver on the day with Mathys on the U20 County Team that won Bronze.

In the U12s, Leah O'Toole had a superb race to finish in 25th, with Ciara Ward close behind in 31st.  Leah and Ciara each scored on the Galway team that came 5th.
